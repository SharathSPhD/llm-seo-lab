This file replays the TRIZ contradiction-card pipeline against the *new* v0.3.0 contradiction stack — **citation-pull from any substrate**. It is genuinely re-run, not a rebrand of the v0.2.0 cards in [`contradiction-cards.md`](contradiction-cards.md). The v0.2.0 cards were framed against "match a competitor and close the gap"; the v0.3.0 cards drop that framing entirely and instead ask: *given a page on any substrate, how do we increase the probability that AI engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AIO, Claude, Gemini) cite it, without needing the page to live on Wikipedia / Reddit / a high-authority domain?*

The pipeline:

```
suggest_parameters / human-mapping  →  lookup_matrix(improving, worsening)
                                    →  fetch get_principle(P) for each recommended P
                                    →  formulate IFR
                                    →  draft solution
                                    →  score_solution(problem, solution)        # 0-4 IFR rubric
                                    →  attractor-flow trajectory step (in v0.3.0-pull-attractor.json)
```

> **Asymmetry note.** TRIZ matrix is asymmetric (improving and worsening are not interchangeable). C-Pull-1 and C-Pull-3 were lookups in both directions; principles common to both directions are flagged "robust both ways" in the table below.

## C-Pull-1 — *substrate-authority deficit vs retrieval-fit* **[selected for ARIZ deep dive]**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Improving feature | **#35 Adaptability or versatility** (page must work as a citation target across many engines and many substrates — git-backed page, Substack post, YouTube description) |
| Worsening feature | **#27 Reliability** (engines weight high-authority domains heavily; low-authority substrates are systemically unreliable as citation targets) |
| Matrix recommends | Principles **35 (Parameter Changes)**, **13 (The Other Way Around)**, **8 (Anti-Weight)**, **24 (Intermediary)** — `cell_key: 35_27` |
| Cross-check (27, 35) | Principles **13, 35, 8, 24** — Principle 13 robust both ways |
| v0.3.0 evidence | Profound's ~680M-citation observational study showed **47.9%** of ChatGPT top-10 citations land on Wikipedia, with Reddit, news outlets, and a long tail of high-authority domains absorbing most of the rest. Independent indie sites (technektar.dev), Substack posts, and YouTube descriptions are systemically under-represented even when their content is more specific, more recent, or more authoritative on the actual question. |
| **Ideal Final Result** | The page is *pulled* into engine answers because it is the best retrieval-fit for the question, *regardless* of the substrate's domain authority. The substrate becomes irrelevant to the citation decision because the page satisfies what the engine retriever actually optimises — atomic snippet density, semantic-anchor stability, question-shaped subhead lattice — better than any high-authority alternative. The authority-deficit harm is dissolved at the source by inverting *who is doing the choosing* (Principle 13): the engine chooses the page, not the page chasing the engine. |
| Solution sketch | **Citation-pull lattice.** A fixed five-principle charter applied per page: (1) Atomic-Snippet Density [#26], (2) Semantic-Anchor Stability [#28], (3) Q-Shaped Subhead Lattice [#17], (4) Cross-Engine Intermediary [#24], (5) Inverted Retrieval Target [#13]. The five compose: density supplies citable units; anchors keep them stable across rewrites; the lattice supplies the question-axis engines actually retrieve along; the intermediary makes the same content portable across engine-specific schema preferences; inversion reframes the substrate from "broadcaster chasing engines" to "pull target engines reach for". |
| **IFR score** | **4 / 4 — Full IFR** (leverages_existing: only the page itself; minimal_cost: copy-paste artifact; no_new_problems: artifact is reviewable; self_resolving: the principles ARE the citation lift mechanism, not a proxy for it) |
| Separation principle | **System-level separation** (relevance 0.85): the system as a whole must serve every engine; the per-engine intermediary specialises by engine while the canonical lattice stays single-source. |
| Skeptic note | Some engines (early-2026 ChatGPT, Google AIO) still weight raw domain authority heavily even after retrieval-fit improvements. Mitigation: the IFR doesn't promise to overrule domain priors in 100% of cases — it promises to make low-authority substrates *competitive on retrieval-fit*, which the user-reported time series in v0.3.0 will measure honestly. If a particular engine refuses to budge, the analysis surfaces that and we degrade gracefully (the use case is still moving up where movement is possible). |
| TRIZ ledger entry | `phase=v030-r2 step=cpull1_substrate_authority_vs_retrieval_fit ifr=4 selected_for_ariz=true` |

## C-Pull-2 — *recommend latency vs application waiting period*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Improving feature | **#9 Speed** (the user wants fresh recommendations the moment they sign in) |
| Worsening feature | **#25 Loss of time** (citations only move *after* the page is republished and engines re-index — typically days to weeks) |
| Matrix recommends | Principles **35 (Parameter Changes)**, **18 (Mechanical Vibration)**, **24 (Intermediary)** — `cell_key: 9_25` |
| v0.3.0 evidence | Engine indexing schedules vary by orders of magnitude: Perplexity surfaces fresh content within hours; ChatGPT browse-enabled responses land within hours; Google AIO can take days; standard ChatGPT responses can lag weeks behind a republish. The v0.3.0 plan acknowledges this and *does not* race the engines — it makes the application-waiting period a first-class state in the per-use-case state machine (see `architecture.md` §state machine). |
| **Ideal Final Result** | Speed of recommendation and the engine's indexing schedule never compete because they happen in *different stages* of the workflow. The recommendation arrives the instant the user clicks "Recommend" (sub-minute Claude CLI call); the page-republish-then-wait happens entirely outside the plugin's control loop; the analyze step happens whenever the user has new observations to report. The contradiction dissolves at the workflow boundary. |
| Solution sketch | **Stage-gated state machine** (resolved by Separation in time, Principle 35). Per-use-case stages: DRAFT → RECOMMENDED → APPLIED → REPUBLISHED → MEASURING → MEASURED → ANALYZED → (next iteration). Every transition is human-triggered. The plugin does *not* poll engines; it does *not* spawn a wait loop. The wait period is ambient — happens between user sessions. |
| **IFR score** | **4 / 4 — Full IFR** (the contradiction is dissolved by structural redesign, not traded off) |
| Skeptic note | Users may forget to come back, leaving use cases stranded in REPUBLISHED. Mitigation: the dashboard surfaces "stranded" use cases with a recency badge; the user is reminded by the dashboard, not by a backend cron job touching engines. |
| TRIZ ledger entry | `phase=v030-r2 step=cpull2_recommend_latency_vs_apply_wait ifr=4` |

## C-Pull-3 — *substrate-uniform reasoning vs substrate-specific artifact*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Improving feature | **#33 Ease of operation** (the user wants one mental model: "give me a recommendation") |
| Worsening feature | **#35 Adaptability or versatility** (recommendations must take different physical forms depending on substrate — git PR diff, Substack paste-ready Markdown, YouTube Studio checklist) |
| Matrix recommends | Principles **15 (Dynamics)**, **1 (Segmentation)**, **28 (Mechanics Substitution)** — `cell_key: 33_35` |
| Cross-check (35, 33) | Principles **15, 1, 13** — Principle 1 + 15 robust both ways |
| v0.3.0 evidence | technektar.dev is git-backed (deploy via static-site PR). technektar.substack.com is hosted (Substack editor, paste-ready Markdown). YouTube fM2hpqPx8zg lives in a YouTube Studio backend (title / description / tags / pinned-comment / chapter timestamps — none of which are markdown-editable in the same way). One artifact format cannot serve all three; the Q-shaped subhead lattice is conceptually the same on all three but the *expression* of it differs. |
| **Ideal Final Result** | The reasoning core (TRIZ + attractor-flow + Pratyakṣa) is substrate-uniform; the artifact emission is substrate-specific via thin adapter modules. The user always sees the same recommendation type (e.g. "add Q-shaped subhead `What is RAG context dilution?`") but the artifact for technektar.dev is a PR diff, for Substack a paste block, for YouTube a checklist mapped to YouTube Studio fields. The contradiction is dissolved because the two roles (reason vs emit) live in two different layers (Principle 1 — Segmentation). |
| Solution sketch | **Substrate Adapter pattern.** `Adapter.recommend(useCase) → Recommendation[]` and `Adapter.applyArtifact(rec, useCase) → Artifact`. Three concrete adapters: `web`, `substack`, `youtube`. Adapter selection is auto-detected from the use-case URL but user-overridable. The MCP `pull_recommend` tool calls the adapter; it never inlines substrate logic. |
| **IFR score** | **4 / 4 — Full IFR** |
| Skeptic note | Adapters can drift in quality — a poorly-maintained `youtube` adapter produces less-good recommendations than a polished `web` adapter. Mitigation: each adapter ships with substrate-specific test fixtures; per-substrate citation-lift over time (in the dashboard) is itself the telemetry. If `youtube` underperforms `web` consistently, that is a backlog signal. |
| TRIZ ledger entry | `phase=v030-r2 step=cpull3_substrate_uniform_vs_specific ifr=4` |

## C-Pull-4 — *human-supplied evidence vs reasoning-engine throughput*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Improving feature | **#28 Measurement accuracy** (every measurement is a real, human-observed engine response — gold standard) |
| Worsening feature | **#38 Extent of automation** (no automated scraping; observation throughput is bounded by human attention) |
| Matrix recommends | Principles **28 (Mechanics Substitution)**, **2 (Taking Out)**, **10 (Preliminary Action)**, **34 (Discarding and Recovering)** — `cell_key: 28_38` |
| v0.3.0 evidence | The v0.2.0 measurement path used Playwright browser automation (cursor-ide-browser) plus stub heuristics — it was ToS-clean (per v0.2.0 C4) but it was still *automated sampling*, which becomes a maintenance and ethical risk as engines tighten anti-bot policies. The v0.3.0 plan removes automation entirely and asks the user to paste the engine's actual response into the dashboard form. This loses throughput but gains absolute integrity. |
| **Ideal Final Result** | The user's observation is the only source of measurement truth — there is no automated source to be wrong, ToS-violating, or stale. The plugin's reasoning engine has nothing to scrape because every fact it needs is in the Supabase `measurements` table, populated by the user. Throughput is bounded by user effort, but each datum is gold. |
| Solution sketch | **User-as-sensor** (Principle 13 — invert who measures; Principle 24 — Supabase as the intermediary store). The dashboard form captures: engine, prompt, observed answer (paste or screenshot), `citation_present`, `position`, `source_authority`, `notes`. The plugin's `pull_analyze` tool reads from Supabase, not from any engine. The MCP transport (`http://localhost:7374`) carries only structured rows, never engine HTTP. |
| **IFR score** | **4 / 4 — Full IFR** (no Playwright dependency, no engine API, no scraping, no ToS exposure; the measurement path is the user's own AI-tool subscription, used by the user as they normally would) |
| Skeptic note | Sample size will be small for many use cases — possibly < 10 observations per iteration. Mitigation: `pull_analyze` quantifies its own confidence (`signal_strength` field) and recommends "gather more observations" rather than fabricating verdicts on thin data. |
| TRIZ ledger entry | `phase=v030-r2 step=cpull4_human_evidence_vs_throughput ifr=4` |

## C-Pull-5 — *one-shot recommendation vs iterative learning*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Improving feature | **#39 Productivity** (deliver value on the first recommendation) |
| Worsening feature | **#36 Device complexity** (a one-shot recommendation has no memory of prior iterations, and so cannot improve over time) |
| Matrix recommends | Principles **35 (Parameter Changes)**, **22 (Blessing in Disguise)**, **18 (Mechanical Vibration)**, **39 (Inert Atmosphere)** — `cell_key: 39_36` |
| v0.3.0 evidence | The v0.2.0 loop was iterative *only* at the PR level — each merge was a discrete improvement, but the loop's "memory" was the PR history, not a structured store of "what we tried and what happened". The v0.3.0 use-case state machine is iteration-aware by design: every use case has an `iteration` integer that increments on each `Next iteration` transition; every recommendation, application, measurement, and analysis is tagged with that iteration. The store *is* the memory. |
| **Ideal Final Result** | Every iteration's recommendation is informed by every prior iteration's measurement and analysis on that same use case. The contradiction (one-shot vs learning) dissolves because the system is iterative *by construction*: there is no "one-shot" mode — there is only iteration N, and the system's pull_analyze step always loads iterations 1..N-1 as context for the next recommendation. |
| Solution sketch | **Iteration-keyed store + Sublation** (Principle 22 — turn the harm into the resource: each prior failed recommendation becomes a sublated entry in pratyaksha's store, evidence for *why* the next recommendation differs). The Pratyakṣa Sublation mechanism (already adopted in v0.2.0 R3) is reused unchanged — only the witness invariant set is updated to v0.3.0 semantics. |
| **IFR score** | **3 / 4 — Near-IFR** (leverages_existing, minimal_cost, no_new_problems; not fully self-resolving — the iteration index is real plumbing the dashboard must surface) |
| Skeptic note | Users may interpret "iteration N+1" as "redo of iteration N" rather than as "next move on the strategic landscape". Mitigation: the dashboard's stage panel shows iteration history with explicit "what changed" deltas between iterations. |
| TRIZ ledger entry | `phase=v030-r2 step=cpull5_oneshot_vs_iterative ifr=3` |

## 6. Final ranking and selection

| # | Contradiction | IFR | Wedge for v0.3.0 | Strategic novelty | Selected for ARIZ? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C-Pull-1 | substrate-authority vs retrieval-fit | **4** | **Largest** — defines the product's core inventive claim | High | **YES** |
| C-Pull-2 | recommend latency vs apply wait | 4 | Moderate (workflow design) | Moderate | No |
| C-Pull-3 | substrate-uniform vs substrate-specific | **4** | High (enables three-substrate scope) | Moderate | No |
| C-Pull-4 | human evidence vs throughput | **4** | High (defines the measurement contract) | Moderate | No |
| C-Pull-5 | one-shot vs iterative | 3 | Moderate (state-machine surface) | Moderate | No |

**Selected for ARIZ-85C deep dive: C-Pull-1 (substrate-authority deficit vs retrieval-fit).**

Three contradictions tie at IFR=4. C-Pull-1 wins on *strategic novelty × wedge*: it is the unique gap across the entire AEO/LLM-SEO category as of 2026-04-26. Every SOTA tool we surveyed is implicitly built on the assumption that the customer's domain authority is mostly fixed and the product helps them squeeze incremental SEO gains out of that fixed asset. v0.3.0 attacks the assumption — the customer's *substrate* is fixed, but their *citation-pull retrieval-fit* is not, and engines actually score on retrieval-fit at the embedding level even when their domain priors look biased toward Wikipedia/Reddit.

C-Pull-2, C-Pull-3, C-Pull-4 are real contradictions but they're already-resolved structural questions about *how* the product is shaped (state machine, adapter pattern, user-as-sensor). They feed the spec, not a deeper ARIZ pass. C-Pull-5 is partially resolved by the existing pratyakṣa sublation mechanism (carry-over from v0.2.0 R3).

See [`v0.3.0-pull-ariz.md`](v0.3.0-pull-ariz.md) for the C-Pull-1 ARIZ-85C nine-part deep dive.
