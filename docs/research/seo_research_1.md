# seo in the ai era is changing drastically...there is news that meta is hiring seo talent while laying off other staff..same with other companies...the reasoning is that google search and page rank no longer matter with ai chatbot surfaced results and the criteria that chatbots use are quite different from traditional seo..research this entire changing landscape...primarily looking at commercial impact for companies and how ai tools/companies are reorienting for that...the key is to surface the changing technology ...what traditional seo and marketing was and what is changing due to ai...and how are the ai companies positioning the seo technology...and also how do they intend to monetise

SEO isn’t dying, but it’s being rewired: the centre of gravity is shifting from “ranked blue links in Google” to “being cited and recommended inside AI answer engines,” which is changing hiring, traffic patterns, tooling, and monetization models across the ecosystem.[^1][^2]

Below is a structured walkthrough of what’s changing, how the tech works, and how AI players plan to make money from it.

***

## What traditional SEO looked like

Traditional SEO focused on ranking pages in search engine results pages (SERPs) using levers like keyword targeting, link building, technical hygiene, and content length/coverage.[^1]
The primary success metric was position (e.g., top‑3 rankings) and associated organic click‑through rate, with content often long‑form and keyword‑rich to capture as many relevant queries as possible.[^3][^1]

This model assumed that search engines mainly acted as routers: they showed snippets, then sent users to publisher sites where monetization happened via ads, lead gen, or e‑commerce.[^3]

***

## How AI is changing search behavior and technology

AI systems like ChatGPT, Gemini, and agentic tools are turning search from “list of links” into “direct answers and multi‑turn conversations,” meaning users often get what they need without ever clicking through.[^2]
Google’s AI Overviews (formerly SGE) now generate synthesized answers at the top of many result pages, capturing attention and reducing clicks to traditional organic and paid listings when they appear.[^4][^5]

Studies of AI Overview rollouts show measurable drops in click‑through rate for both organic results and ads on SERPs where an AI answer is present, especially on mobile where the AI box dominates the viewport.[^6][^5]

***

## Commercial impact on traffic and funnels

Experiments on AI Overview SERPs report average organic CTR declines around 8–9% when an AI Overview is present, with ad CTR in some tests dropping from roughly 21% to under 10%.[^5]
Agencies analyzing millions of queries find that as AI summaries occupy more top‑of‑page real estate, both paid and organic placements are pushed down, reducing visibility and making each remaining premium slot more competitive and expensive.[^7][^6]

For many informational and early‑funnel queries, AI now “absorbs” the intent: users get summaries and comparisons without clicking, which weakens the traditional model where publishers monetize pageviews and email capture at the top of the funnel.[^2][^4]

***

## How the SEO job market is shifting

Data from thousands of job listings show SEO roles are not disappearing; instead they’re becoming more technical, cross‑functional, and commercially accountable, with employers explicitly asking for AI and LLM skills.[^8]
One large analysis found AI mentioned in roughly 31% of senior SEO listings and over 20% of other roles, with interest in AI search, AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) and AI Overviews visibility appearing in a growing share of descriptions.[^8]

New titles like “AI SEO Specialist,” “AI Search Specialist,” and “GEO Strategist” focus on making brands both findable in Google and citable in AI answers from tools like ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews.[^9][^10]
At the same time, content‑only SEO roles are under pressure, while strategy/technical leadership roles (e.g., SEO managers and VP‑level roles) are growing and often carry higher salaries.[^11][^8]

***

## Why companies like Meta are still hiring SEO

Meta’s recent posting for a high‑paid SEO Strategy Manager role illustrates this shift: the remit includes advanced technical SEO, large‑scale site migrations, and staying on top of AI‑driven changes in search and “GEO” best practices.[^12]
Broader hiring data show enterprises are still investing in SEO, but expect these hires to connect visibility directly to revenue, understand AI‑driven search journeys, and work closely with engineering, analytics, and product.[^8]

In other words, while some teams are cutting generic content roles, they are upgrading to fewer, more senior SEO leaders who can navigate both classic Google ranking and AI‑era discovery.[^11][^8]

***

## From SEO to AEO/GEO/LLM SEO

A growing body of practice talks about “Answer Engine Optimization” (AEO), “Generative Engine Optimization” (GEO), or “LLM SEO,” all centred on one goal: being the source that AI systems cite in their answers.[^13][^1][^3]
Instead of optimizing only for keywords and backlinks, these approaches prioritise structured, factual, easily parsable content; strong authority signals; and formats that map cleanly to question‑and‑answer extraction by large language models.[^1][^2]

Typical tactics include heavy use of schema (Organisation, Article, FAQ, Product), entity‑centric content, concise factual paragraphs, and monitoring how often brands are cited across AI platforms as a new visibility KPI.[^10][^13][^1]

### Traditional SEO vs AI‑era optimization

| Dimension | Traditional SEO | AI‑era AEO / GEO / LLM SEO |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Primary goal | Rank high in SERPs for target keywords.[^1] | Be cited as an authoritative source in AI answers.[^1][^13] |
| Main surface | Organic listings and snippets on Google/Bing.[^1] | AI Overviews, chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity), agents.[^2][^3] |
| Core levers | Keywords, backlinks, content length, on‑page tweaks.[^1] | Structured data, factual precision, entity clarity, external authority.[^1][^2] |
| Measurement | Rankings, organic sessions, SERP CTR.[^1] | Citation frequency in AI answers, traffic from AI‑driven links, share of answers mentioning your brand.[^1][^13] |
| Risk if ignored | Competitors outrank you on key queries.[^3] | AI systems answer users’ questions without ever mentioning you.[^2][^13] |


***

## How AI platforms decide what to surface

Guides on AEO note that AI answer engines favour content that is highly structured, unambiguous, and fact‑rich, because models need snippets they can safely quote or paraphrase with minimal hallucination risk.[^2][^1]
This means schema markup, clear headings that mirror user questions, and concise factual blocks become strong signals, alongside traditional authority indicators like backlinks and brand mentions.[^3][^1]

At the platform level, deals and partnerships increasingly influence what content AI systems can access and prioritise:

- **OpenAI / ChatGPT** has signed licensing deals with major publishers such as AP, Axel Springer, Le Monde, Financial Times, and others, giving partner content priority placement when ChatGPT Search browses the web.[^14][^15]
- Analyses of citation patterns suggest that licensed, “tier‑one” publishers (e.g., NYT, WSJ, FT) are disproportionately cited in ChatGPT answers relative to similarly high‑quality but unlicensed outlets.[^16]

For non‑partner businesses, the path to visibility runs through being referenced by these authoritative sources, and ensuring your own content is technically accessible and structurally clear when ChatGPT or other systems browse.[^14][^1]

***

## Google’s AI Overviews and monetization strategy

Google’s AI Overviews sit on top of its classic ranking stack: the system still uses search signals and the existing ads auction, but now first generates an AI answer and then chooses which links and ads to show around it.[^17][^18]
Official documentation confirms that existing Search, Shopping, Performance Max, and App campaigns are eligible to show ads above or below the AI Overview, and that ads can also appear inside the AI summary when they’re contextually relevant.[^19][^17]

Data from agencies and experiments show that when AI Overviews appear, text ads often get pushed further down the page, while Shopping units frequently sit above the AI box for commercial queries, especially in retail categories.[^6]
At Google Marketing Live 2025, Google announced expanded ads embedded directly in AI Overviews and in “AI Mode,” its conversational search experience, making paid placements part of the AI dialogue rather than separate slots.[^18][^19]

Practically, Google is nudging advertisers toward AI‑driven targeting like broad match, Performance Max, and AI Max for Search, because eligibility for AI Overview inventory increasingly depends on these systems’ ability to match ads to both the query and the AI‑generated content.[^17][^18]

***

## Perplexity’s different path: from ads to subscriptions \& rev‑share

Perplexity initially experimented with sponsored answers and shared advertising revenue with publishers, allocating ad revenue based on whose content was cited in AI responses.[^20][^21]
Publisher partners such as Time, Der Spiegel, The Independent and others received revenue shares tied to citations and user visits, aligning monetization with the AI answer surface itself.[^20]

However, in early 2026 Perplexity publicly abandoned advertising, citing concerns that sponsored content could undermine trust in AI answers, and repositioned subscriptions as the backbone of its business.[^21][^22]
Reports suggest Perplexity now generates roughly 200 million dollars in annualised revenue primarily from Pro, Education Pro, and enterprise plans (around 20 to 200 dollars per month), with a free tier as a funnel.[^23][^21]

For companies, this means visibility in Perplexity answers is not currently pay‑to‑play; it depends on being a high‑quality source that the system chooses to cite, while publishers can earn via rev‑share schemes like Comet Plus that allocate subscription revenue based on citations and agent actions.[^21][^20]

***

## OpenAI’s publisher deals and emerging “AI search” economy

OpenAI has been building a publisher partnership program where outlets license their content in exchange for compensation and preferential citation in ChatGPT Search.[^14]
Partners include large news and magazine brands like AP, Axel Springer, Le Monde, Financial Times, Vox Media, and others, with deals designed to provide up‑to‑date, authoritative material for ChatGPT’s browsing mode and emerging search products.[^15][^14]

Separate analysis of Reddit’s AI licensing deals estimates that OpenAI pays on the order of tens of millions of dollars annually for access to Reddit’s Data API, alongside Google’s own roughly 60‑million‑dollar per‑year agreement.[^16][^15]
These licensing arrangements not only solve copyright and recency issues, they effectively create an “AI paid content tier” where licensed sources are over‑represented in AI answers and become new power brokers in the discovery ecosystem.[^15][^16]

***

## Meta and AI‑driven performance marketing

While Meta doesn’t run a classic web search engine, it is aggressively using AI to automate ad buying and optimization across its social surfaces, which changes how brands get discovered there.[^6]
Analyses comparing Google’s SGE with Meta’s AI Ads note that Meta is moving toward heavily automated, AI‑managed campaigns that allocate budget and targeting dynamically, often outperforming manual setups in conversion rates and ROI.[^6]

From a commercial standpoint, this makes Meta less about organic SEO and more about feeding the right creative and signals into a black‑box AI ad system, while still relying on a smaller number of technical SEO leaders to manage web properties and organic discovery.[^24][^12]

***

## What this means for companies in practice

1. **SEO isn’t optional, but its scope has expanded**
Employers increasingly expect SEO leaders to understand not just Google rankings, but also AI search behaviour, AI Overviews, AEO, and LLM visibility, and to tie all of this to revenue.[^10][^8]
2. **The goal shifts from “ranked” to “referenced”**
Winning now means:
    - Being structurally easy for AI to parse (schema, entities, short factual blocks).[^1][^2]
    - Being authoritative enough that AI systems (or their licensed partners) trust and cite you.[^16][^1]
3. **Traffic mix and attribution must adapt**
Companies are starting to track “AI citations,” traffic from AI‑driven links, and brand mentions in AI answers as new KPIs alongside traditional rankings and organic sessions.[^13][^1]
One AEO case study reported a SaaS company increasing AI mentions by around 200% within six months after restructuring content for AI‑friendly extraction.[^1]
4. **Publisher economics are fragmenting**
Some publishers lean into licensing and rev‑share (OpenAI, Perplexity, Microsoft Copilot), while others consider blocking crawlers or negotiating higher‑value deals, because classic search referrals are eroding.[^20][^15][^16]
This shapes which sources AI systems can legally and practically rely on—and therefore which brands get downstream visibility.
5. **Monetization is bifurcating**
    - Google is weaving ads directly into AI answers and conversational search, preserving an ad‑funded model but with new rules for eligibility and placement.[^19][^18][^17]
    - Perplexity is betting on subscriptions plus publisher rev‑share and explicitly stepping away from ads to protect perceived trust.[^22][^21]
    - OpenAI is building a licensed content ecosystem where it pays for access and leverages that to power ChatGPT Search and related products, monetized via subscriptions and potentially future commercial surfaces.[^15][^14][^16]

For commercial teams, the net effect is that “SEO” becomes a hybrid discipline: part classic search optimization, part data/structuring for AI engines, part partnership strategy with platforms and publishers that increasingly control which content models see—and cite—in the AI era.[^8][^2][^1]
